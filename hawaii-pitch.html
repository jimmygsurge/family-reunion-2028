The Lodging Play

The Hyatt anchor is real — if we book it like insiders.

The family's anchor idea — Hyatt Vacation Club at Ka'anapali Beach (the Hyatt Residence Club) — is a 131-unit beachfront residence resort with full kitchens, in-unit laundry, a zero-entry lagoon pool, and Ka'anapali Beach out the front door. Booked at retail, 9 nights for 20 people runs $35,000–$50,000+. Booked as owner rentals through RedWeek and Koala — renting timeshare owners' weeks directly, with RedWeek's verified-and-protected escrow holding our money until check-in — the same villas land at roughly $22,000–$33,000 (2028 est.). That's the whole strategy: same beach, same pools, same kitchens, roughly half price.

Two clean configurations for 20: two 3-bedroom villas (sleeps 10 each — exactly 20) or three 2-bedroom villas (sleeps 6–7 each). Grandparents get their own suite either way. Booking path: owner-rental inventory for summer 2028 typically appears about 12 months out — set alerts on the RedWeek rentals page in summer 2027 and move fast; summer Maui weeks in big units go first.

Full transparency, up front: timeshare weeks rent in fixed 7-night blocks (usually Sat–Sat). For 9 full days we'd build a 10-night trip from a 7-night owner-rental week plus 2–3 flexibly booked nights (Hyatt direct, Maui Resort Rentals, or a night in Hana mid-trip) — or simply run two consecutive weeks. It's a solvable puzzle, but it's real, and the other pitches don't have it.

Kid Freedom Report

Can the five roamers (ages 6, 6, 9, 9, 11) run loose? Honest answer: mostly.

A beachfront resort complex hands the middle kids real independence: pools, lawns, and the beachwalk path all inside one property, with the shave-ice stand and Whalers Village a flat stroll away. The 11-year-old can absolutely run ahead to the pool or the shave-ice line alone; the 9s roam in cousin pairs; the 6s stay within sight of a big kid or an adult. But we'll be straight: this is a public resort, not a sealed compound — it's roam-with-eyes-on, not true free-range. An Alaska cruise ship (a closed vessel with kids' clubs) or a fully private villa estate genuinely beats us on kid independence, and we won't pretend otherwise.

What tips it back our way: the condo-cluster setup. With the family's 2–3 units in one complex, the cousins free-flow between doors — knock, grab a cousin, hit the pool — which is the reunion behavior everyone actually wants. And since the family has said separate units with shared amenities are fine, our 2–3-unit beachfront cluster is no longer a knock at all. The one thing a cluster can't give is a single big living room for all 20 — so we book the largest unit (a 3BR) as the HQ commons: game nights, the movie pile, and the big group dinners happen there.

Nine Days, Every Generation

The Top 5 — with real group costs for 12 adults (13+), 6 paying kids, and 2 free under-4s.

1. Molokini Crater + Turtle Town snorkel — aboard the Calypso

~$187/adult • ~$127/child 4–12 • ages 3 & under free → group ≈ $3,000–$3,300 + tax/tip (the baby AND the 3-year-old ride free)

A triple-deck glass-bottom catamaran with a waterslide and easy stair entry from the swim platform — non-snorkelers ride in comfort, the littlest ones watch fish through the glass floor. 150-foot visibility inside a volcanic crescent. Morning departures only; the trades chop the water after 1 pm. (Trilogy is the premium option at ~$231/pp.)

2. Luau night — Old Lahaina Luau (or budget swap)

$229/adult 13+ • $140/child 3–12 • under 3 free → group ≈ $3,730, baby free (Myths of Maui swap: ≈ $2,850)

Widely rated the most authentic luau in Hawaii — oceanfront, four-course dinner, open bar for the adults. It sells out months ahead; with 20 people we call group sales directly. Want fire-knife spectacle instead? Drums of the Pacific is next door to our Hyatt villas (~$202+/pp).

3. Ka'anapali Beach + Black Rock — the free daily anchor

$0 — buy snorkel sets at Costco Kahului (~$35/set) instead of renting at $10/day

Turtles at Black Rock most mornings, the nightly cliff-dive torch ceremony, and a stroller-friendly 3-mile oceanfront beachwalk linking resorts, restaurants, and Whalers Village — all out our front door. For the littlest three (18 months, 3, and 4): Kapalua Bay and Napili Bay, 15 minutes north, are reef-protected crescents with gently sloping sand — the calmest little-kid swimming on the island, and the zero-entry lagoon pool back at the villas is made for exactly this trio.

4. Haleakala summit — sunset for the group, sunrise for the brave

$30/vehicle entry (+$1 sunrise reservation) → 4 vehicles ≈ $120–$124 total

A 10,023-foot volcano above the clouds. Real talk: sunrise means a 2:30 am wake-up, 40°F at the summit, and a Recreation.gov reservation that sells out in ~15 minutes, 60 days ahead. So the teens-and-parents squad chases sunrise; everyone else gets identical views at sunset with no reservation and no meltdowns.

5. Road to Hana — the smart "half-Hana" split

Self-drive ≈ $300–$600 (fuel + park fees) • guided vans for 12 ≈ $2,500+

620 curves and a 10–14 hour day is a no for an 18-month-old, a 3-year-old, and a 20-person convoy — so we don't pretend otherwise. The adventurous sub-group does the full drive (or a guided van, so nobody white-knuckles it); everyone else does the front half to Twin Falls and Ke'anae and is back for a beach afternoon. Wai'anapanapa's black-sand beach needs a timed reservation (~$10 parking + $5/person).

Straight talk on whales: humpbacks visit Maui December–April. In July there are no whales — anyone promising whale watching in summer is selling you something. Our summer wins are the warmest, calmest ocean of the year and near-zero rain. Rain-day backup: Maui Ocean Center aquarium (~$45 adult / $35 child ≈ $750 group).
The Budget Superpower

The kitchen math: how villas pay for the beach.

Yes — Hawaii groceries run 40–60% above the mainland. We're not hiding that. But every unit in our plan has a full kitchen, and Costco Kahului is 10 minutes from the airport (20–30% below Hawaii supermarkets). Feeding 20 people in restaurants three meals a day would cost $100–$150 per person per day. Cooking breakfasts, lunches, and most dinners in — with 3–4 proper restaurant nights out — cuts the food bill by more than half.

The $35K Build

What $35,000 actually buys on Maui — because the dream build runs $48.5–65k.

If the family votes with the wallet, here's the honest budget version, all-in ex-airfare:

  • Lodging ~$17,000–$20,000: Westin Ka'anapali Ocean Resort Villas owner rentals at the low end (3×2BR lockoffs, sleeps 24, same beach, better waterslides) — or Wailea Ekolu 4×2BR (~$18.5k) if we trade beachfront for the sunny south side.
  • Food ~$8,500–$9,500: the groceries-heavy kitchen plan, trimmed to two restaurant nights instead of four.
  • Wheels ~$4,500–$6,000: 2–3 minivans instead of 4, incl. gas and parking.
  • Activities ~$3,200–$4,000: free-beach-first itinerary (Black Rock, Kapalua Bay, the beachwalk), Haleakala sunset (~$120), and ONE paid marquee day — Molokini (~$3,000) OR the luau (~$3,730), not both.

Total ≈ $33,000–$39,000. What gets cut vs. the dream build: the Hyatt anchor itself (Westin instead), one of the two marquee splurges, two restaurant nights, the fourth van, and guaranteed-oceanfront views. What survives untouched: the beach, the cousins-in-one-complex setup, the kitchens, the sunsets — the actual reunion.

Credibility Section

The real pros and cons — including the ones that hurt our pitch.

The family asked for honesty. A pitch that hides its weaknesses deserves to lose. Here is everything, unvarnished:

Why Maui genuinely wins

  • Zero passports for 20 people. The only tropical option where an expired or lost passport can't strand a family. With 9 kids, this is not a small thing.
  • Shortest flights for 16 of 20: the Salt Lake eleven on one Delta nonstop (<7 hrs, baby free on a lap), the Oceanside five on a 5.5-hr San Diego nonstop — half the effective travel of Europe. The four Boston/NH travelers connect once.
  • Near-guaranteed weather: 85–88°F, ~0.35" rain all July on our coast, 80°F calm ocean — summer is West Maui's flattest, safest swim season.
  • Every age wins: reef-protected toddler bays (Kapalua, Napili, Honokowai), waterslide pools, teen snorkeling and surf lessons, a 3-mile oceanfront beachwalk, luau nights for everyone.
  • Full kitchens save $10,000–$15,000 vs. restaurant-feeding 20 — the deepest condo/timeshare stock in the U.S. tropics makes it possible.
  • U.S. everything: healthcare, cell plans, car seats, pharmacies, English — meaningful when you're traveling with nine kids, including an 18-month-old.
  • The owner-rental market makes the Hyatt anchor affordable — roughly half of retail, with escrow protection, and a same-beach Westin fallback if inventory tightens.
  • Activity depth: Molokini, Black Rock, Haleakala, Hana — nobody is bored on day 6, and the biggest daily activity (the beach) is free.

The honest downsides

  • The $30k target only works via the owner-rental market. Retail beachfront for 20 in summer is $35k–$50k+. If we won't do RedWeek/Koala, our budget buys garden-view Wailea condos or the Westin — not the Hyatt.
  • 7-night timeshare blocks complicate a 9-day trip. We'll stitch a 10-night stay from a 7-night week + 2–3 flexible nights, or run two consecutive weeks. Solvable, but it needs early commitment (watch listings from summer 2027).
  • Realistic all-in is ~$48,500–$65,000 ex-airfare (≈$2,400–$3,250/person). Maui is one of the priciest destinations in the U.S. — the Big Island would save $10k+, and we said so above.
  • Groceries run 40–60% above the mainland (milk $6 even at Costco, $8–10 at Safeway). The kitchen plan beats restaurants, but food is still a five-figure line.
  • Hidden cost we found: Hyatt parking is $40/night per vehicle — 4 vans × 10 nights = $1,600. Mitigation: keep 2–3 vans, not 4. Honua Kai adds a $40/nt/unit resort fee; timeshares add ~$25–38/day/unit tax.
  • No whales in summer. Humpbacks are Dec–Apr. If whale watching is a must-have, this is the wrong season — full stop.
  • Summer = peak crowds and peak minivan chaos: observed van rates swung $750→$2,080/week in one summer. Book refundable early, re-shop monthly.
  • Lahaina town is still rebuilding. Ka'anapali resorts are fully open and the harbor and Old Lahaina Luau operate, but historic Front Street's core likely remains under reconstruction into 2027–2029. We'll be respectful guests in a recovering community.
  • Kid roaming is eyes-on, not free-range. It's a public resort: the 11-year-old and the 9s can run to the pool and shave-ice stand, but a cruise ship or private villa compound beats us on true kid independence. And with 2–3 units instead of one roof, there's no single big living room — we mitigate by making the largest unit the group HQ.
The Closing Argument

Why Hawaii wins this vote.

Greece is beautiful — for the 11 adults. Alaska is spectacular — through a window. Slovenia is a gem — 24 hours of travel away. Maui is the only option built for all twenty of us at once: the littlest three splashing at Kapalua Bay, the teens diving Black Rock, the grandparents out on the morning snorkel with them, and every family behind its own kitchen door at night — together at one pool by day.

We've shown you the real numbers, the real fees, and the real trade-offs, because this plan doesn't need hiding. The anchor the family already loves — the Hyatt on Ka'anapali Beach — is achievable near budget through the owner-rental market, with a same-beach fallback if it isn't. No passports. Nonstops for 16 of 20. Guaranteed sun. An ocean warm enough for every generation.

The Vote Card  •  Final Case for #1

"But we've all been to Hawaii before." True. That's exactly the point.

To the voters who crave somewhere new — the grandparents, the New Hampshire wanderer, the no-kids couple — we're not going to pretend Maui is novel. Instead: familiar is a feature when you're bringing an 18-month-old and eight other kids. You already know Hawaii delivers — which means zero risk of a rained-out, over-budget, jet-lagged reunion, and every ounce of energy spent on each other instead of on logistics triage. And be precise about what's actually new here: you've been to Hawaii, but never all 20 of you at once, never in one beachfront compound on Ka'anapali with your own kitchens and one shared pool. Nobody in this family has ever taken that trip. That trip is new.

The ease case seals it: nonstops for 16 of 20, the baby free on a lap for a 6.5-hour domestic flight instead of 15+ hours to Europe, no passports for anyone, and the guaranteed-great week the other three pitches can only gamble on. The cousins free-flow between our units to the shared pools, the middle kids get real (eyes-on) run of a beachfront resort — and if the budget vote tightens, the $35K Build above keeps us in Hawaii without losing the beach.

Full honesty: this is the priciest option on the ballot and the least novel. If novelty alone decides your ranking, Maui loses — we're betting the reunion is about something else.
