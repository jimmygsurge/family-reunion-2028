What each option is actually best at

🏛️ Greece — the classic villa reunion

Strongest card: the full fantasy, in budget — one 9-bedroom villa sleeping exactly 20, warm sea, sandy toddler-grade beaches, tavernas, a private boat day to Balos Lagoon. Lodging is $12k under target, funding a chef and the charter.

Biggest bite: the most expensive airfare of the five ($26–36k group) and 15–19 hours each way with an 18-month-old. July heat is real; the pool needs a rented fence.

🚢 Alaska — the effortless togetherness play

Strongest card: nobody cooks, nobody drives, nobody splits up — one ship carries all 20 past calving glaciers, then the Denali extension. Cheapest flights of the five, and the only option where grandma and grandpa are fully off duty.

Biggest bite: $30k buys only the lean version (~$53–57k all-in; $69–75k done right). The 18-month-old is below every cruise line's kids-club and pool line (no swim diapers allowed), and the 3-year-old needs to be potty-trained for club access — so the baby's parents are on duty at sea.

🏔️ Slovenia — the value-and-substance play

Strongest card: the cheapest trip of the five by a wide margin (~$39–50k all-in), and new to 19 of 20 of you with arguably the best activity density — lake, castle, caves, rafting, alpine toboggan, all within an hour of one 20-person house with a 20-seat table.

Biggest bite: the hardest flights (16–20 hrs, everyone connects), essentially one true 20-sleeper (real booking risk), no private pool, and no warm sea.

🌺 Hawaii — the path of least resistance

Strongest card: no passports for anyone, nonstops under 7 hours for 19 of 20, the calmest toddler ocean, and near-guaranteed perfect weather. The safest choice — nothing about it can really go wrong.

Biggest bite: everyone in the family has already been to Hawaii — the lowest-novelty option on the ballot. It's also the most expensive trip ($60–84k all-in), the $30k lodging target only works via timeshare owner rentals in fixed 7-night blocks, and the family splits across 2–3 units rather than one roof.

🌲 North America — the certainty play

Strongest card: the lowest travel bill on the ballot (16 of 20 can drive to Tahoe — group travel ~$3–4k vs $12–36k of airfare everywhere else), road-trip naps for the 18-month-old, lakefront cabins with docks and acres where the cousins genuinely free-range, and a trio structure that keeps three doors open — including Banff, the single biggest "wow" on the entire ballot. The $35k test fits with surplus for boats and a nanny.

Biggest bite: Tahoe and Coeur d'Alene won't out-exotic Crete or the fjords for the new-experience voters, late-summer wildfire smoke is a real regional wildcard (early July is clearest), and choosing Banff gives back most of the travel-ease advantage.

🌊 Norway — the jaw-dropper

Strongest card: the only option that is brand-new to all 20 of you — with scenery none of the others can match, cool comfortable hiking temps, 19-hour days, and kids riding free nearly everywhere. The $30k lodging budget nearly covers the whole trip on the ground.

Biggest bite: weather is a genuine gamble (Bergen: ~16 rain days in July), daily prices are brutal ($650+ group dinners), the water is 57–61°F, and true 20-sleepers are nearly nonexistent.

The referee's verdict

The family's own criteria, applied: ease of travel matters most to the three families with kids; new and unique experiences matter most to the grandparents, the singles, and the no-kids couple. Run every option through those two filters and the field sorts itself. Hawaii takes the biggest hit — it's the easiest trip but the only one everyone has already taken; familiarity is its comfort and its ceiling. Norway climbs — new to all 20, the most jaw-dropping scenery of the five — but it asks the kid-families to absorb 13–16 travel hours. Greece threads both needles: a one-day cruise stop years ago doesn't spend Crete's novelty (a villa week on the other side of the island is a different trip entirely), and once you've survived the flight, daily life there is the easiest of the international options — one villa, one beach, no moving. North America owns the ease axis outright and adds the family's new "kid freedom" test to its column: on private lakefront acres — or a cruise ship — the 6-to-11s roam in a way no hotel allows. And on the same-$35k test, the value options get vivid: Slovenia and Tahoe/Coeur d'Alene buy the whole trip plus a nanny and a chef, while Maui and full-fat Alaska can't squeeze in.

If the vote were mine: it still comes down to Greece vs. Slovenia. Greece delivers the one-roof-plus-warm-sea fantasy inside the lodging budget with near-full novelty. Slovenia is new to 19 of 20, roughly $20–25k cheaper all-in than Greece or Hawaii, with arguably the best kids' itinerary — its price is the hardest journey for exactly the families ease matters to. That tension — Greece easier once there but pricier to reach, Slovenia cheaper but harder to reach — is the real question for the family vote.

Choose Hawaii if the deciding voters prize ease over price — it's the easiest trip on the ballot (nonstops for 16 of 20) with the best toddler ocean — but weigh that against it being the one place everyone has already been, at a ~$15–35k premium over the value options. Choose Alaska if the grandparents' rest matters most and the potty-training question resolves — it's the only truly effortless option (nobody cooks, nobody drives), but be clear-eyed that done right it runs ~$69–75k. Norway is the wildcard: the most spectacular and the coolest (literally), best for a family that hikes more than it swims and will trade rain risk for fjords. Choose North America if certainty, cost, and kid freedom outrank exotic — with the trio runoff keeping Banff's wow alive inside the vote.

Cross-cutting logistics (not decision factors, just to-dos): if an international option wins, the 9 kids' passports should be started by mid-2027 (~$1,300–1,500 total) — a chore, not a reason to choose differently. Every option books 12–18 months out for July at this group size, so a decision by late 2026 / early 2027 keeps all doors open. And in every destination, the last week of June beats July: lower prices, thinner crowds, milder heat, and school's out for all nine grandkids.

The family vote — ranked choice

Drag the destinations into your order of preference — top of the list is your first choice (the ▲▼ arrows work too). If no destination wins a majority of first choices, the last-place option is eliminated and those ballots transfer to their next choice, round by round, until one destination has a true majority. No spoilers, no vote-splitting between the two Mediterranean options — just rank honestly. With the 11 adults voting, winning takes 6 votes after transfers. The starting order is shuffled for each voter, so nobody inherits a default.
