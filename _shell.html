What each option is actually best at

🏛️ Greece — the classic villa reunion

Strongest card: the full fantasy, in budget — one 9-bedroom villa sleeping exactly 20, warm sea, sandy toddler-grade beaches, tavernas, a private boat day to Balos Lagoon. Lodging is $12k under target, funding a chef and the charter.

Biggest bite: the most expensive airfare of the five ($28–36k group) and 15–19 hours each way with a 3½-year-old. July heat is real; the pool needs a rented fence.

🚢 Alaska — the effortless togetherness play

Strongest card: nobody cooks, nobody drives, nobody splits up — one ship carries all 20 past calving glaciers, then the Denali extension. Cheapest flights of the five, and the only option where grandma and grandpa are fully off duty.

Biggest bite: $30k buys only the lean version (~$52–55k all-in; $68–74k done right). All 20 need passports including every child, and if the 3½-year-old isn't potty-trained there's no kids club and no pool.

🏔️ Slovenia — the value-and-substance play

Strongest card: the cheapest trip of the five by a wide margin (~$40–50k all-in) with arguably the best activity density — lake, castle, caves, rafting, alpine toboggan, all within an hour of one 20-person house with a 20-seat table.

Biggest bite: the hardest flights (16–20 hrs, everyone connects), essentially one true 20-sleeper (real booking risk), no private pool, and no warm sea.

🌺 Hawaii — the path of least resistance

Strongest card: no passports for anyone, nonstops under 7 hours for 19 of 20, the calmest toddler ocean, and near-guaranteed perfect weather. The safest choice — nothing about it can really go wrong.

Biggest bite: the most expensive trip ($62–84k all-in), the $30k lodging target only works via timeshare owner rentals in fixed 7-night blocks, and the family splits across 2–3 units rather than one roof.

🐋 Portugal — the two-worlds trip

Strongest card: two vacations in one — volcanic crater lakes, Furnas hot springs, and year-round resident sperm whales on São Miguel (the direct answer to Maui's no-whales problem), then a warm Algarve week in one 10-bedroom villa that sleeps all of you. Boston gets the shortest US–Europe flight in existence (5-hr nonstop to Ponta Delgada), and Portugal's daily costs are among Western Europe's lowest.

Biggest bite: moving 20 people mid-trip costs a full day plus ~$5–6.5k, Azores weather is a roulette even in July, no single 20-sleeper exists on São Miguel, and the all-in total (~$68–85k) lands in Hawaii territory.

🌊 Norway — the jaw-dropper

Strongest card: scenery none of the others can match, cool comfortable temps that are gold for the grandparents, 19-hour days, and kids ride free nearly everywhere — the $30k lodging budget nearly covers the whole trip on the ground.

Biggest bite: weather is a genuine gamble (Bergen: ~16 rain days in July), daily prices are brutal ($650+ group dinners), the water is 57–61°F, and true 20-sleepers are nearly nonexistent.

The referee's verdict

If the vote were mine: it comes down to Greece vs. Slovenia, and the tiebreaker is what "reunion" means to this family. If it means one villa, one pool, warm sea, and everyone at the same long dinner table every night — Greece wins: it's the only option that delivers the full one-roof-plus-beach fantasy inside the lodging budget. If it means maximum shared adventure per dollar and nobody stressing about cost, Slovenia wins — it's roughly $20–25k cheaper all-in than Greece or Hawaii, and the kids' itinerary is arguably the best of the five.

Choose Hawaii if the deciding voters prize ease over price — it's the only option with zero passports, the shortest flights, and the best toddler ocean, at a ~$15–30k premium over the value options. Choose Alaska if the grandparents' rest matters most and the potty-training question resolves — it's the only truly effortless option, but be clear-eyed that done right it runs ~$68–74k. Norway is the wildcard: the most spectacular and the coolest (literally), best for a family that hikes more than it swims and will trade rain risk for fjords. Portugal is the variety play — whales, volcanoes, and a warm villa week in one itinerary, and by far the easiest trip for the Boston brother — but it stacks Hawaii-class totals on top of a 20-person mid-trip move, so choose it for the two-worlds experience, not for simplicity or savings.

Cross-cutting facts for the family vote: five of six options need passports for all 20 — start the 9 kids' applications by mid-2027 (~$1,300–1,500 total). Every option books 12–18 months out for July at this group size, so a decision by late 2026 / early 2027 keeps all doors open. And in every destination, the last week of June beats July: lower prices, thinner crowds, milder heat, and school's out for all nine grandkids.
