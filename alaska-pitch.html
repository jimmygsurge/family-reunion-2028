The core argument

A ship is the only "one big house" that actually fits this family

Every competing pitch — the Greek islands, the Slovenian villa, Maui — has to solve the same brutal problem: how do 20 people spanning 64 years of age eat together, sleep near each other, and still each have a good day? Villas split into car caravans. Island-hopping means ferries with a toddler. A resort means three condos and a group text about where to meet.

A cruise ship dissolves the problem. Seven cabins on the same corridor. Free kids' clubs by age band, a teen lounge, a covered pool, quiet decks for the grandparents — and every single night, two adjoining tables of ten at the same fixed dinner seating, no reservation scramble, no driving, no cooking rotation. The scenery moves so nobody has to.

The recommended plan

Princess "Voyage of the Glaciers" northbound + DIY Denali

Late June 2028 · Vancouver → Whittier (Anchorage), 7 nights · then 3 land days to Denali National Park. Princess and Holland America are the one-way Gulf of Alaska specialists; Princess's route is the only 7-night itinerary that includes both Glacier Bay National Park and College Fjord — two full scenic-cruising days — plus Ketchikan, Juneau, and Skagway. Northbound means the trip ends in Alaska, exactly where the Denali extension and the flights home begin.

The highlight reel

Five experiences the family will still talk about in 2048

1

Glacier Bay + College Fjord — two full glacier days included in fare

Park rangers board the ship; calving ice thunders off a 250-foot wall while all 20 of you stand at the same rail. The toddler needs nothing but a jacket; the teens will film it. This is the trip's beating heart, and it costs zero extra.

2

Juneau humpback whale watching $123–$230/person · ~$2,800–3,200 group

Near-100% sighting rates in peak season on heated, enclosed boats — genuinely toddler-safe and teen-approved. A group of 20 can often charter an entire small boat to itself.

3

Skagway: White Pass & Yukon Route Railroad $155 adult / $77.50 child · ~$2,300–2,600 group

A vintage narrow-gauge train climbing 3,000 feet over Gold Rush trestles, boarding steps from the pier. Zero walking required — the single best all-twenty-ages excursion of the trip.

4

Ketchikan: Lumberjack Show & totem poles ~$40–60/person · ~$1,000 group

Cheap, hilarious, one hour, steps from the ship. Teens who want more adrenaline can swap in a rainforest zipline (~$189).

5

Denali Tundra Wilderness Tour $144.75 adult / $66 child · ~$2,350 group

Grizzlies, caribou, moose, and Dall sheep from the park's classic wildlife bus, deep past where private cars can go. (Honest note: it's 6–7 hours — one parent may sit it out with the toddler at the lodge's free sled-dog demo.)

Days 8–10

The Denali extension — do it ourselves and save thousands

Day 8: Walk off the ship in Whittier, motorcoach to Anchorage (~$65/person), pick up 3 minivans + an SUV. Lunch stop in Talkeetna (Denali views, riverside town the kids will love), drive on to the park entrance.
Day 9: Tundra Wilderness Tour into Denali National Park; evening at the lodge.
Day 10: Scenic drive back, final night in Anchorage, fly home from ANC.

Full disclosure

The real pros and cons — including the ones that hurt

Why it works

  • The one-roof factor is unbeatable. Seven cabins, one corridor, dinner for 20 at two adjoining tables every night — no villa, resort, or island-hop can match it with zero driving and one unpack.
  • Every age has a lane. Free kids' clubs (ages 3–17 in bands), a teen lounge, a covered pool, quiet adult decks — parents actually get a vacation too.
  • Two included glacier days — the wow moments cost nothing extra and happen with everyone together at the rail.
  • 2028 timing is perfect: the grandkids will be 3½ to 16 — old enough to remember it, young enough to still all come.
  • Cool-weather comfort: 60s–70s°F, 18–20 hours of daylight, no sunburned toddlers, easy on the grandparents.
  • Roughly half Disney's price for a better itinerary, and the land leg ends at Anchorage's airport with nonstops toward all three home cities.

Where it genuinely hurts

  • Passports for all 20 — including every child. One-way Vancouver sailings void the birth-certificate exception. ~$1,300–1,500 and applications must start by late 2027. Miss one passport, miss the ship.
  • The potty-training cliff. If the 3½-year-old isn't fully potty-trained by June 2028, mainstream lines allow no kids-club drop-off and no pool access — swim diapers are banned fleet-wide. Only Disney (2× the price) and Royal Caribbean truly solve this.
  • Motion sickness risk is real. The final Gulf of Alaska crossing is open ocean and can roll noticeably. Mitigate with midship cabins and Bonine, but anyone badly prone to seasickness will feel it.
  • Open-jaw airfare costs ~$5,000–7,000 more than a simple round trip, and three cities converging on Vancouver demands a buffer day.
  • Weather is a coin flip. Juneau averages 64°F with regular drizzle; Denali's summit hides ~2 days out of 3. Pack rain shells, not swimsuits — there is no beach on this vacation.
  • $30k realistically buys only the lean version. The full recommended experience is $52–60k ex-air. Excursions for 20 add up fast ($6,500 for the full slate), and 7 connecting cabins require booking the day 2028 inventory opens.
The closing argument

Why Alaska wins the family vote

Greece is gorgeous — and it's ferries, heat, and four rental cars with nine kids. The Slovenian villa is charming — and someone cooks for 20 every night while the teens ask what there is to do. Maui is easy — and it's three condos, one pool, and a week that looks like every other resort week.

Alaska is the only pitch where the hotel itself does the traveling. Twenty beds move together past calving glaciers while the family sleeps, and every generation wakes up to the same jaw-dropping view — then splits into kids' club, teen lounge, whale boat, and quiet deck, and reunites at the same dinner table by seven.

The grandparents get comfort and grandeur without a single long walk. The teens get whales breaching and a gold-rush railroad. The little ones get moose, sled dogs, and a ship to explore. The parents get actual rest. And in 2048, nobody will say "remember that villa?" — they'll say "remember when the glacier cracked and the whole family screamed at once?"

One booking window, one corridor of cabins, one unforgettable map line from Vancouver to Denali. That's the reunion. Vote Alaska.
