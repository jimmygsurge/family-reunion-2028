Family Reunion 2028 · The North America Pitch

Three mountain lakes.
No redeyes. One easy vote.

This ballot line is a trio: Lake Tahoe, Coeur d'Alene, and Banff–Canmore. Vote "North America" now, and the family holds a second, smaller vote later to pick among the three. What you're really voting for today: nine days together without overnight flights, customs lines, or an 18-month-old melting down at an international gate.

2 of 3 options need no passports
~$3,500 group travel to Tahoe vs $14k–$36k airfare abroad
16 of 20 can drive to Lake Tahoe (Utah + California crews)
All 3 have real 20-person lodging under ~$30k

The shared argument: the trip starts at the driveway, not the gate

Every international option on the ballot spends its first day — and $14,000–$36,000 of group airfare — just getting twenty people through airports. The North America option spends that day already at the lake. Our family is 11 Salt Lakers, 5 Oceansiders, 3 Bostonians, and one New Hampshirite — and for Lake Tahoe, both the Utah and California crews (16 of our 20) simply drive: gear in the trunk, car seats installed, the 18-month-old napping on the highway instead of over the Atlantic. The four New Englanders fly ordinary domestic daytime flights — no redeyes, no customs. No passports for Tahoe or Coeur d'Alene. No overnight travel for anyone. And the airfare money doesn't vanish — it becomes a bigger house, a boat day, and a college-fund-sized savings line.

A vote for North America is a vote for a second, easier decision

You are not choosing between Tahoe, Coeur d'Alene, and Banff today. You're choosing the continent. If North America wins the ranked-choice vote, the family runs a quick runoff among these three — with full pricing below so that vote takes ten minutes, not ten email threads.

Option 1 · California / Nevada

Lake Tahoe — North Shore (Truckee · Tahoe Donner · Tahoe Vista · Incline Village)

The bluest big lake in America, sitting almost exactly between the two driving hubs: ~8 hours from Salt Lake City on I-80, ~8–8.5 hours from Oceanside up I-15/US-395. Sixteen of our twenty arrive by car; the four New Englanders fly into Reno, 35 minutes from the cabin.

A Tahoe Donner big-home lot gives the pack real ground of its own, and the HOA is built for exactly this: the olders can roam to the Trout Creek Rec Center (three pools) and the private Donner Lake beach on a buddy system, and quiet forest trails and bike loops start at the back door with easy "stay inside the neighborhood" boundaries. Honest ledger

For

  • The only option BOTH driving crews can drive — 16 of 20 arrive by car (SLC ~8 hrs, Oceanside ~8–8.5 hrs), and the flyers land just 35 min from the cabin
  • ~$3,150–3,900 total group travel; no passports, ordinary domestic flights
  • Deep 18–20-person cabin inventory; lodging lands under the $30k target
  • 75–82°F and ~90% sunshine in late June/July; mild 6,000–7,000 ft elevation

Against — said plainly

  • The water is cold. Tahoe runs ~60–68°F even in July. Kids will swim anyway; adults will wade. (Donner Lake and Sand Harbor shallows run warmer.)
  • Wildfire smoke is a real lottery ticket. The 2021 Caldor Fire evacuated South Lake Tahoe in late August. Late June/early July is historically the clearest window, but a bad year can gray the views. Trip insurance is in the budget.
  • South Lake Tahoe's VHR permit cap rules out most big homes in city limits — the plan must stay North Shore / Truckee (it does).
  • July peak crowds: lake-loop traffic, full beaches, reservation systems.

Honest ledger

For

  • Warmest water of the trio — ~70–73°F in July. This is the one where everyone actually swims off the dock all day.
  • True lakefront 18–20-person lodges with private beaches — the "one big house on the water" dream is most real here
  • Silverwood + Hiawatha is arguably the strongest pure-kid one-two punch on the whole 2028 ballot
  • Cheapest lodging of the three; mid-80s°F and dry in July

Against — said plainly

  • Lowest wow-factor of the trio. It's gorgeous, but it's not Emerald Bay and it's certainly not Moraine Lake. Nobody frames a Coeur d'Alene photo next to a fjord.
  • Oceanside can't drive it — 9 of 20 fly (vs 4 for Tahoe), plus two rental vehicles, so the travel bill runs ~$5,000–6,500 (still ~75–85% below the international options)
  • Silverwood in mid-July is a genuine tourist crush — plan a weekday, arrive at opening
  • Utah's 10-hour drive usually becomes an 11–12-hour day with kids, or an overnight split
  • North Idaho shares the Northwest's August–September smoke season; late June/July is usually clear, not guaranteed
Option 3 · Alberta, Canada

Banff & Canmore — the showstopper (with a price of admission)

The most spectacular mountain scenery in North America, full stop: Moraine Lake, Lake Louise, the Icefields Parkway. It keeps the no-jet-lag card (Mountain Time, same as Utah) — but it surrenders the drive-there card: this one means flights and passports for everyone who goes.

The honest note: some of us think of Banff as "closer" because it's the same time zone and a short flight from SLC. Time-zone-wise, true — no jet lag. Cost-wise, it behaves like an international trip: its travel bill overlaps the low end of the Greece/Hawaii airfare range. The savings vs. Europe come mostly from the favorable exchange rate on the ground, not from getting there.

Where 20 people sleep — the honest inventory problem

Canmore restricts short-term rentals to "tourist home" zoned properties, and as of March 2025 no new tourist homes can be created — so true 20-person single houses are scarce and the realistic play is one rare big lodge or a cluster of condos/suites. That's fine by this family's own rules — separate cabins or condo clusters with shared amenities are explicitly acceptable, and a big shared living room is a celebrated bonus where a property has one, not a requirement. Inside Banff townsite it's even tighter (federal park lease rules). All totals: 9 nights, USD, incl. taxes/fees, 2028-adjusted.

Kid Freedom Report — the six roamers (6, 6, 9, 9, 11, 11)
Grade: Limited — the honest weak spot

The condo-cluster reality means shared courtyards and a pool rather than private acres, so day-long free-ranging shrinks to supervised outings. The consolation is real: Canmore's compact townsite is genuinely walkable, and the 9s and 11s can run bakery, playground, and candy-store missions on their own. But this is bear country — kids don't wander trails alone here, ever, and that rule is non-negotiable. Families who rank kid freedom first should weigh this heavily against Banff's scenery card.

Honest ledger

For

  • The biggest single "wow" on the entire 2028 ballot. This family has already done Hawaii (everyone), Crete (a cruise day each), an Alaska cruise (one), and Slovenia (one). Nobody has seen Moraine Lake. For the voters who care most about NEW — the grandparents, the no-kids couple, the solo traveler — Banff out-postcards everything else per dollar spent on the ground, Norway included
  • No time-zone pain for 16 of 20: Mountain Time, same as Utah; short nonstops from SLC and San Diego
  • Exchange rate makes meals, groceries, and activities ~25–30% cheaper than the sticker suggests
  • July weather 70–75°F, cool nights, wildflowers; trail options at every length — the grandparents will happily out-hike half the group here

Against — said plainly

  • It sacrifices the premise. The North America pitch is "most of us just drive" — and Banff realistically means flights for all 20, at ~$11,000–$15,250 in airfare plus $3,500–5,000 in rentals (Utah driving 13 hrs each way is the only cost escape hatch).
  • Passports for all 20, including all 9 kids — not a dealbreaker, just a real to-do: ~$2,800 in fees and one apply-by-January paperwork errand across five households
  • True 20-person homes are scarce — Canmore's tourist-home zoning is frozen; expect a condo cluster (fine by the family's rules) with shared courtyards instead of private grounds — the real cost lands on kid roaming, not on togetherness
  • Crowds are legendary — Moraine/Louise access is a reservation war, parking lots fill before 7am, July is peak of peak
  • No swimming. The famous lakes are glacial — 40–50°F. Beautiful to look at; nobody's doing cannonballs off a dock.
  • Canada's recent record fire seasons (2023, Jasper 2024) mean smoke risk here too

The Case, In One Card

Rank North America #1 — keep three doors open

For the kid-families: the dream logistics

Road-trip naps beat redeyes when you're traveling with an 18-month-old and a 3-year-old. For Tahoe, 16 of our 20 just drive — car seats stay installed, the pack-n-play rides in the trunk, nobody clears customs at midnight. And once there, the six 6-to-11s get real freedom: private grounds, a dock or a rec center, cousins running loose from breakfast to dinner.

The lowest travel bill on the ballot

~$3,150–3,900 gets the whole group to Tahoe, versus $14,000–$36,000 in airfare for the international options. The difference buys the lakefront lodge, the boat day, and the theme-park day — combined.

For the NEW-experience voters

Grandparents, the solo traveler, the no-kids couple: Banff is inside this vote too — and it would be the single biggest "wow" on the entire ballot. Nobody in this family has seen Moraine Lake. (The passports are a to-do, not a dealbreaker.)

You're not picking a place yet

This vote chooses certainty and ease — then the family picks the flavor together: warm-water Coeur d'Alene, drive-there Tahoe, or showstopper Banff. Three doors stay open; every other ballot line is one door.

Full honesty: Tahoe and Coeur d'Alene will never out-exotic Crete or the fjords — they win on ease and togetherness instead.
